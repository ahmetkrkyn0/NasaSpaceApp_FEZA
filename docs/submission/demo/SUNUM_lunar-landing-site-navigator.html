HOW NASA DATA MAKES IT WORK

Open NASA data turns a date into a sky you can check

POSITIONS

Sun and Earth from NASA JPL SPICE ephemerides

TERRAIN

Horizon from the LOLA elevation map, NASA PGDA 90

RESULTS

Sunrise, Earthset, power and link windows

Every number carries a label: Measured, Model, Derived or Assumed

04

Positions come from the NAIF SPICE kernels (DE440 planetary ephemeris and the Moon orientation kernels), with light time and observer motion corrections. Terrain horizons for the catalogue sites are computed from the LOLA 20 m south pole elevation map (PGDA 90). Validation compares against NASA Horizons and the PGDA 69 average solar visibility map. Results are computed on the server only; the interface shows them with their validity label.

VALIDATION

No reference check fails in the validation run

Sunlit time over a year: 44.98 % here, 46.46 % on the NASA map

▸ in our test scenario · approximate check

06

The validation run checks Sun and Earth angles at six past landings and the Mons Mouton December events against NASA Horizons, power for four panel types, horizon dip and the event time sensitivity. The yearly sunlit share at Mons Mouton is compared with the NASA PGDA 69 map; the map is an 18.6-year average at 120 m per pixel, so this is an approximate comparison, not a pass or fail.
